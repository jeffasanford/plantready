1. Name the units and equipment

Hiring managers want to know what you ran. "Operated equipment" could mean anything. Name the units, the processes and the equipment you touched every shift.

Weak"Operated equipment and performed rounds."
Strong"Ran outside rounds on the amine, SRU and dehydration units, operating pumps, compressors and exchangers."

2. Show your safety and permit work

Safety work is real experience, so put it on paper. Isolations, LOTO, permits, gas testing, confined space attendant and fire watch all belong on your resume.

Weak"Followed all safety procedures."
Strong"Wrote and verified LOTO isolations and handed equipment over to maintenance under the site permit system."

3. List startups, shutdowns and turnarounds

Turnaround and startup experience is valuable to plants and contractors alike. List each one, the unit, and what you did on it.

Weak"Helped during turnaround."
Strong"Worked the 2024 FCC turnaround: blinding, draining and handing over equipment, then leak checks and startup."

4. Use numbers you actually know

Numbers make your work concrete: years on a unit, number of turnarounds, pieces of equipment handed over, trains or units covered. Only use numbers you can back up in an interview.

5. Put your tickets where they're easy to see

TWIC, Basic Plus (or your site's safety card), OSHA 10 or 30, H2S, first aid and any operator qualifications should sit in their own section. Recruiters often check for them first.

Common mistakes

  • Job titles with no details. "Operator, 2019–2024" tells them nothing about what you ran.
  • One long paragraph. Use 3–5 short bullets per job so it can be skimmed in seconds.
  • Leaving out turnaround and contract work. It counts, even if it was short.
  • Fancy layouts. Columns, graphics and photos can confuse the software many companies use to screen resumes. A clean, one-column layout works best.
  • Making things up. Interviewers will ask about every line. Only write what you can talk about.

Common questions

How long should a process operator resume be?

One page is best for most operators. Two pages is fine if you have 10 or more years and several units or sites worth listing.

What if I don't have plant experience yet?

Lead with your process technology training, safety tickets and any related work such as military, maintenance, manufacturing or turnaround helper jobs. Show the same habits plants want: following procedures, shift work and safety.

Should I include turnaround or contract jobs?

Yes. List them with the site, the unit and what you did. Short contract jobs still show experience that plants and contractors value.

Do I need a different resume for each job?

You should adjust it for each posting. Move the most relevant units, equipment and tickets to the top and use the same words the posting uses.
